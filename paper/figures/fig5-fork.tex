% The fork rule: one function, two versions, and who decides. Prints as
% Figure 4. Single column, drawn beside Section 4.3. Same rails, glyphs and palette as
% Figures 1 to 4. The instance is the one the walkthrough reaches in 5.5.
\newcommand{\FigForkCaption}{Two people change \protect\sym{api.py::handle} from
the version both of them last saved, one taking three lines off the top of it and
one adding a line to the logging block at its end. Git's merge of the two succeeds
and prints nothing, and nobody has run the function it produces in that
state. \sgt{} records both versions, includes neither, and names the function, which
is the only refusal in \sgt{} that can stop work that would have been correct.}

\newcommand{\FigForkDescription}{Three bands. The top band shows one recorded
version of a function splitting into two, one change removing three lines at the
top of the function and one adding a line at the bottom, with the two resulting
function bodies drawn side by side. The middle band shows git's result, a single
function body with the top lines gone and the bottom line added, marked as never
run. The bottom band shows sgt's result, both versions drawn as dotted outlines to
mean neither is included, and one solid version after the developer writes the
merge and the test command passes.}
